CURATE
Product Strategy, Validation Roadmap & Google AI Studio Build Specification
Final consolidated document — October 2026

Working name: Curate. This document consolidates the two supplied source documents and the decisions selected during reconciliation. Core product remains non-AI; optional AI is not on the committed roadmap.
Executive summary

Three biggest opportunities
Own the space between visual curation and actual consumption planning: the product turns saved content into scheduled consumption.
Make time-aware planning the utility layer: weekly available time, duration, drag-and-drop, and a visual calendar give the product a job beyond bookmarking.
Build a calm, non-AI product with deterministic planning. This keeps costs predictable and makes the product feel like a focused tool rather than an AI wrapper.
Three biggest problems
The screenshots communicate save/curate better than why a user should schedule.
The calendar can become a beautiful planning surface without creating a completion or weekly-return loop.
The visual concept invites feature sprawl before product-market signal; social, AI, gamification, complex tagging and large integrations must remain gated.
Reconciled decisions


Part I — Product strategy
1. Product audit
What Curate does
Curate lets users save YouTube videos, articles, podcasts and books into a visual infinite canvas, keep unscheduled items in an Inbox, and then schedule them across a calendar using Day, Week and Month views. The central transformation is from “interesting things I have saved” to “the things I will actually consume this week.”
Who it is for

Core value proposition
Primary: Turn the content you save into a realistic schedule you can actually follow.
Secondary: Curate your interests visually, then decide when you will watch, read, listen, or learn.
Screenshot-based UX findings

Aha moment
Recommended behavioral definition: a user saves 3–5 pieces of content, schedules at least 1, and opens the calendar to see a coherent plan. Target time-to-aha: under 3 minutes.
Secondary activation metric
Also measure whether users who save 5 items and schedule 3 are more likely to return the following week. Do not replace 3–5–1 before collecting evidence.
2. Market & competition
The relevant competitive set is not only calendar software. Curate sits at the intersection of visual bookmarking, read-it-later/reference tools, and personal planning. The opportunity is the gap between these categories.


Category language
Recommended: visual content planner / personal consumption planner / personal learning calendar. Avoid “content calendar” as the primary category because the phrase is strongly associated with social-media publishing.
3. Feature strategy and prioritization

Top 3 immediate product priorities
1. Time Budget + Quick Plan: gives the calendar a concrete job and creates a deterministic “smart” outcome without AI cost.
2. 3–5–1 activation: creates a measurable first success and gets users to the differentiating surface quickly.
3. Messaging + empty state: makes the product’s planning purpose understandable before users need instructions.
4. Monetization

Recommended initial structure


Upgrade triggers
Adding the 101st item.
Needing unlimited history or advanced planning controls.
Using Pro sharing/capture capabilities.
Any future feature that has a clear ongoing-value relationship to an active user.
Do not cripple basic scheduling to force payment. Public beta comes before technical billing.
Unit-economics frame
Source assumption from the no-AI build analysis: variable cost was estimated at about USD $0.4/month per paying user when AI usage is removed. Because the final price is now EUR (BGN) 5/month, actual margin and LTV should be recalculated once infrastructure and billing fees are observed. Do not scale paid acquisition until retention produces acceptable payback.

6. Retention & metrics

Early warning signs
Users save content but rarely schedule it → collection is more compelling than planning.
Users schedule but do not return → calendar is useful once but not habit-forming.
Users complete very little of what they schedule → planning is unrealistic.
Users ask for folders/tags/database features before they use the calendar → positioning may be drifting toward bookmark management.
Users like screenshots but do not return → visual appeal is attracting attention without creating utility.
Cheap validation tests
Five-user usability test: ask people to add three things and plan a week without explanation.
Landing message test: “Save links visually” vs “Turn saved content into a weekly plan.”
Time Budget test: manually run the planning exercise with ten people and observe usefulness.
Completion test: add a simple complete action and see whether users return the next week.
Public collection test: publish three hand-curated artifacts and measure sign-ups.

Part III — Technical build specification
Core architecture principles
Core functionality must not depend on AI or external model APIs.
Prefer deterministic algorithms and free/public endpoints where appropriate.
Keep operating costs predictable.
Keep API keys server-side.
Keep APP_NAME in one constant so the working name can change in one place.
Reuse existing components and preserve routes/data/drag-and-drop behavior.
Do not redesign unrelated screens during feature work.
Before every implementation change, AI Studio must first propose a plan in five bullets or fewer, list assumptions, then implement.
After every change, AI Studio should explain how to test it in under two minutes.
Back up/duplicate the project before larger architectural changes.
Data model baseline
The core content item should at minimum support: id, url, type, title, source, imageUrl, durationMin, priority, status, plannedDate, createdAt, metadataFetchedAt. User settings should include weeklyHoursAvailable, theme and planning preferences. Public sharing must use separate unguessable IDs/tokens and never expose private library data.
Analytics with Umami
Use Umami for privacy-conscious web analytics and a small first-party product event log for behavioral events. Core events: item_added, item_planned, item_completed, first_run_activation, weekly_review_opened, share_created, share_imported, upgrade_viewed, purchase_completed.
Security baseline for future real-link capture
Allow only http/https article fetches.
Block private/local IP ranges.
Use short timeouts and strict response-size limits.
Limit redirects.
Sanitize fetched text before rendering.
Never make an external source or API key the canonical record; the source URL is canonical.
Cache metadata only as long as product/API rules allow and revalidate current platform policies before launch-scale use.

Part IV — Sequential Google AI Studio prompts
Use one prompt at a time. Paste the standing rules first, let the agent provide its plan and assumptions, read the plan, then let it implement. Run the test line after each stage. If a change breaks unrelated behavior, tell the agent to revert the last change. Do not ask AI Studio to rebuild the whole product in one shot.
Standing prompt — paste before every stage
STANDING RULES. Apply to this change and every later one.
- App: Curate, a visual planner for videos, articles, podcasts and books. Keep the current look: dark editorial design, serif italic titles, jewel-tone cards, light/dark toggle.
- Make only the change described below. Do not redesign, rename or remove other features.
- Do not add any AI or LLM feature and do not call the Gemini API.
- Do not add a paid service without asking me first. Prefer free public APIs and built-in browser features.
- Keep the app name in one constant (APP_NAME) so it can be renamed in one place.
- Keep every API key and secret server-side. Never put them in client code.
- Before coding, give me a plan in 5 bullets or fewer and list assumptions. Then implement.
- When done, tell me how to test the change in under 2 minutes.
- Reuse existing components. Preserve routes, existing data, drag-and-drop and working features.
- Do not add generic SaaS dashboard patterns, chatbots, AI badges, gamification or unnecessary onboarding.

Prompt 1A — Quick UI fixes
Make these small fixes on the existing screens. Change nothing else.
1. Home: the hero headline and paste bar must never overlap or clip at widths from 1024px to 2560px. Keep the paste bar centered and visible.
2. Cards: use one source field consistently on the card face and caption.
3. Raise the smallest card-face and metadata text to at least 12px and meet WCAG AA contrast in dark and light themes.
4. Titles: wrap on word boundaries and truncate with an ellipsis after two lines.
5. The type icon must follow the actual item type.
6. Add a text wordmark using APP_NAME = 'Curate'.
Test: resize Home from 1024px to 2560px, switch themes, and inspect card type/source/title rendering.

Prompt 1B — Messaging and first-use experience
Improve the first-use experience without changing the overall visual design.
Headline: Everything worth exploring. On your schedule.
Supporting copy: Save YouTube videos, articles, podcasts and books, then plan when you'll actually consume them.
Primary input placeholder: Paste a YouTube, article, podcast, or book link…
Primary action: Save
Empty state: Start with 3 things you want to consume this week.
Actions: + Add a video / + Add an article / + Add a book
Do not add a carousel, AI messaging, fake user data, or generic onboarding dashboards.
The product should remain understandable from the interface itself.

Prompt 1C — 3–5–1 activation flow
Build a lightweight first-use activation flow.
Target activation: 3 saved items → 1 scheduled item → Calendar viewed.
After saves, show a subtle 1 of 3 / 2 of 3 / 3 of 3 progress state.
After the third item, offer: Your week is ready to plan.
Primary: Plan my week. Secondary: I'll do it manually.
Do not force a tutorial. Do not use AI. Keep the flow subtle and editorial.
Also instrument the activation milestone for product analytics.

Prompt 2 — Time Budget + deterministic Quick Plan
Build Time Budget and Quick Plan as one combined planning feature.
1. Week view shows AVAILABLE, PLANNED and REMAINING time.
2. Presets: 30m, 1h, 2h, 3h, 5h, 7h, 10h, Custom.
3. Each item exposes duration, type, priority toggle, scheduled state and completion state.
4. If the plan exceeds the budget, show OVER BUDGET but never block the user.
5. Quick Plan must be deterministic and explainable. Use weekly budget, duration, priority, content age, type and existing calendar.
6. Default behavior should automatically select a sensible set while leaving roughly 10–20% of budget free when possible.
7. Provide an Advanced option for planning rules: oldest first, shortest first, or alternate by type.
8. Always preview the proposed schedule before applying it.
9. Allow drag-and-drop edits, cancel and undo.
Do not use AI or call any model.

Prompt 3 — Completion system
Add lightweight completion states: Saved, Scheduled, In Progress, Completed, Skipped.
Allow users to mark scheduled content complete.
Completed items become visually quieter but remain visible.
Week view shows a subtle completed count and consumed time.
Do not add streaks, XP, badges or gamification.

Prompt 4 — Inbox and backlog management
Improve the Inbox for larger collections.
Filters: All, Videos, Articles, Podcasts, Books; Unscheduled, Scheduled, Completed.
Duration filters: <15m, 15–30m, 30–60m, 1h+.
Sorting: Newest, Oldest, Shortest, Longest, Priority.
Add one simple Priority toggle, not a task-manager priority system.
Show an Inbox summary such as 23 unplanned / 7h 42m total.
Button: Plan 2h → opens Quick Plan.
Do not introduce folders, nested databases, complex tagging or AI categorization.

Prompt 5 — Weekly Review
Add a Weekly Review at the beginning of a new week.
Show previous-week totals, Completed, Still interested, and Didn't get to.
For unfinished items: Keep / Reschedule / Remove.
Primary action: Plan next week.
Use existing content and Time Budget.
Do not generate AI commentary or motivational copy.

Prompt 6 — Accounts and persistence after validation
Add accounts and cloud persistence only after the first product-validation gate has passed.
Choose the simplest supported free-tier backend inside AI Studio Build. Propose the backend, expected cost at 1,000 users, and required setup before implementation; wait for my OK.
Support Google sign-in and email-link sign-in.
Keep guest mode with browser-local data. After sign-in, offer to move guest data into the account.
User data: items and settings. Enforce user-level security rules.
Add account menu actions: Export, Sign out, Delete account.
Export JSON and CSV. Delete requires confirmation.
Add Privacy and Terms pages with clearly marked placeholders for storage and third parties.
Never expose private data through public sharing.

Prompt 7 — Share My Week
Add Share My Week.
A Week-view button creates an unlisted public read-only page with a random unguessable ID.
It shows Monday–Sunday, card cover/title/source/duration/type and total planned time.
Owner controls: choose week, hide items, turn link off, delete link.
No name or email unless the owner voluntarily adds a display name.
Add Open Graph/Twitter meta tags and a 1200×630 preview image.
Anyone with the link can view. Saving/importing into Curate requires an account.
Add noindex and rate-limit share creation.
Test in a private browser and through a messaging-app link preview.

Prompt 8 — Share Collections
Add Share Collections after Share My Week is stable.
A user can select content and create a public collection with title, optional description, visual cards, source, duration and type.
The public page should have a Save to my Curate action that requires an account before importing.
Never expose private calendar data or unrelated items.
Do not add likes, comments, followers, feeds or social profiles.

Prompt 9 — Public-beta monetization
Prepare simple Free and Pro entitlements for public beta.
FREE: up to 100 saved items, Canvas, Inbox, Day/Week/Month calendar, manual scheduling, Time Budget, Quick Plan and completion tracking.
PRO: unlimited items, unlimited collections, advanced planning, Weekly Review, Share My Week, Share Collections and future capture features.
Price hypothesis: EUR (BGN) 5/month or EUR (BGN) 39.99/year.
Do not add payment processing until public-beta usage has been observed.
Do not add AI credits or AI pricing.
Use natural upgrade moments, never dark patterns. Lapsed users retain their existing data and exports.

Prompt 10 — Later: Sessions for long content
Do not implement until core planning and retention are validated.
For items longer than 45 minutes, add Split into sessions.
Default session length: 30 minutes for books, 45 minutes for videos/courses.
Create linked session cards with session numbering and progress.
Daily and weekly totals count session minutes, not the full parent duration.
Books may optionally record page ranges.
Support rescheduling, deleting a session and merging back into one item.
Test a 3-hour book split into six 30-minute sessions.

Prompt 11 — Later: Real-link capture
Do not implement until user behavior validates that saving real content is the primary friction.
Support YouTube and articles first, then podcasts/books.
YouTube: server-side oEmbed for title/channel/thumbnail and YouTube Data API for duration. Keep API key server-side and cache metadata according to current YouTube policy.
Articles: fetch Open Graph/Twitter metadata on the server with strict SSRF protections, short timeout and response-size limit.
Failure must never block saving. If metadata fails, save the URL and allow manual Edit details.
Use real images where available; keep the current generated poster as fallback.
Do not call AI.

Prompt 12 — Later: Faster capture
Only build after real-link capture is stable.
1. Multi-link paste: URLs separated by new lines or spaces → one Inbox card each.
2. Public YouTube playlist import with preview and selection; skip duplicates.
3. Bookmarklet / URL parameter for saving the current page into Inbox.
4. Everything lands in Inbox first.
Do not build a full browser extension yet and do not store browsing history.

Prompt 13 — Post-90-day: Calendar integration
Only build after retention evidence shows users repeatedly plan weeks.
Add Download this week as .ics.
Add a private subscribed calendar feed with a secret token that can be regenerated.
Use all-day events because planning is day-based, not time-of-day based.
Description should include source, duration and original link.
Completed items should be clearly marked.
Build for everyone first; paid gating can come later.

Prompt 14 — AI policy / future experiment
Do not implement now.
Curate must remain fully functional without AI.
Only revisit AI if user research repeatedly identifies a planning problem that deterministic Quick Plan cannot solve.
Any future AI feature must be opt-in, explicit, rate-limited, replaceable and off by default.
Do not add an AI chatbot, AI navigation, automatic AI tags, routine summaries or AI pricing.
If an AI feature is ever tested, send only the minimum metadata required and provide a visible off switch plus a hard usage cap.
The deterministic Quick Plan remains the default and fallback.


Final operating rules
1. Do not add features because they look good in the interface; add them because they strengthen SAVE → ORGANIZE → PLAN → CONSUME → COMPLETE.
2. Run user tests at gates. Do not use feature completion as the reason to move to the next phase.
3. Preserve Curate’s current dark editorial identity. Make it more opinionated, not more ornamental.
4. Keep AI out of the core. Do not assume AI is required for intelligence, recommendations or planning.
5. Build persistence only after early validation. Do not spend heavily on infrastructure before the behavior is proven.
6. Use Umami and first-party events to learn what users actually do.
7. Public launch should happen after Share My Week is stable and the activation/retention behavior is understood.
8. Public launch comes before technical billing; monetize after evidence of repeat use.
9. Use EUR (BGN) 5/month and EUR (BGN) 39.99/year as the starting pricing hypothesis, then measure willingness to pay.
10. Calendar sync, real-link capture, Sessions and any AI should remain gated behind evidence.
